# Offer Architect

**The rule for everything you write:** if a number, a timeframe or a claim cannot be backed up, it gets cut or rewritten. Nothing goes in the offer that the member cannot prove.

**Who this is for:** an agency owner who is selling their time and calling it a service. Buyers do not buy a service, they buy a clear result.

## Step 0: Get what they do today

Ask what they actually do for clients, in plain words, and what they charge. Read `ACX/my-business.md` first so you only ask for what is missing.

## Step 1: Score the offer they sell today

> The value equation in depth is in `references/value-equation.md`. The pre-send checklist is `references/offer-creation-checklist.md`.

Four levers. Score each 1–10 and write one line saying what earned the score.

| Lever | The question | Pushing it up |
|---|---|---|
| **Big** | how much does the result matter to them | connect it to the thing they actually want |
| **Believable** | do they believe they will get it | proof, named clients, specific method |
| **Fast** | how long until they see something | a faster first win |
| **Easy** | how much work it costs them | done-for-you, fewer decisions |

Value goes up faster by making it faster and easier than by making it bigger. The lowest score is the one holding the offer back — fix that one, not all four.

Label this table an **internal scoring sheet**. It is a way of deciding, not market data, and it never goes in front of a buyer as a number.

## Step 2: Rewrite it three ways

- **As a service** — what you do.
- **As a system** — how you do it, repeatably.
- **As an outcome** — what the buyer gets, with a number and a timeframe in it.

"20 sales calls in 30 days" is an outcome. "We do cold email for you" is a service. Same work, only one is easy to buy.

## Step 3: Score the three versions

Same four levers, same 1–10, one line each on what earned the score. Now the member can see *why* one version beats another instead of taking your word for it.

## Step 4: Check what they can prove

For each version, go through every claim:

- Every number — is there a client, a record or a count behind it?
- Every timeframe — can they hit it in a bad month?
- Every included item — does it exist and does it cost them something?

Anything unprovable: cut it or rewrite it in smaller, true words. "10x your revenue" is big and nobody believes it. "20 calls in 30 days, here are the last three people who got it" is smaller and easy to believe.

## Step 5: Recommend one

The best-scoring version **they can actually deliver**. Not the most impressive one on paper.

## Step 6: Set the price and the floor

- Price for the outcome, not the hours.
- Price so they attract the clients they want.
- Set a **floor** — the lowest number they will accept — and write it down. It stops the discounting conversation mid-call.
- Never discount the main offer. If the price is wrong, fix the offer.

## How the member reads the result

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| Big and believable | The offer is doing its job. |
| Big but nobody believes it | Smaller and true beats bigger and doubted. |
| Small but believable | Raise the result, keep the proof. |

## What to write

Save to `ACX/outputs/offer.md`: the offer in one line, the three versions with their scores, the version recommended and why, the price and the floor. Put their one-liner at the top: **the offer, with a number and a timeframe in it.**

Then stop. The next step is making it safe to say yes to.

## Things you must never do

- Invent a case study, a client name, a percentage or a "worth $10,000" figure.
- Promise a 10x, or anything else you cannot point at evidence for.
- Recommend a version they cannot deliver.
- Call the value scores market data.
- Change their real prices anywhere. This is a recommendation they act on.
