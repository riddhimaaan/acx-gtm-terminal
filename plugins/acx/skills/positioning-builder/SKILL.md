# Positioning Builder

**The rule that matters most:** if a line could be pasted onto a competitor's site and still fit, it is not positioning. Delete it.

**Who this is for:** an agency owner whose buyers do not doubt they can do the work — they doubt they are any different from the next agency. That doubt is what loses deals they should have won.

## Step 0: Load what is already decided

Read `ACX/my-business.md`. You need who they sell to, their offer and their guarantee — all decided already. Only ask for what is missing.

## Step 1: Find what they believe

Ask, in their words: **what do you believe about your market that most people in it do not?** If they cannot answer, ask what they disagree with that everyone else in their space repeats. Their own words beat anything you would write for them.

## Step 2: Fill the five blanks, in order

Order matters — each one limits the next.

1. **What would buyers use if you did not exist?** Not just competitors: doing it in-house, a spreadsheet, a freelancer, doing nothing. Name the real alternatives.
2. **What do you have that those do not?** Be specific. "Easier to work with" is not a thing. "We run the whole thing for you, you approve the list and read the replies" is.
3. **What does that let the buyer do?** Turn the thing into an outcome they care about.
4. **Who cares most about that?** The group who finds it urgent, not the group who finds it nice. Narrow is stronger.
5. **What category makes it obvious?** Pick the frame that makes their strength matter.

## Step 3: Pick the category on purpose

If they do not choose what buyers compare them to, buyers choose for them — and they choose "another agency", which leaves price as the only thing to compare. Choose deliberately.

## Step 4: Write the statement and three one-liners

The statement: the category they compete in, and the belief that separates them. It is internal — it lines up the thinking. The **one-liner** is what people actually hear.

Write three one-liners, different angles. Not three wordings of the same one.

## Step 5: Test each one-liner twice

1. **The swap test.** Paste it onto a competitor's site. Does it still fit? Then it says nothing.
2. **The stranger test.** Would someone who does not know their business repeat it back correctly after hearing it once? If not, it is too clever or too vague.

Rewrite until both pass.

## Step 6: Cut the words everyone uses

Go through the statement and the one-liners and delete anything every competitor already says. Whatever is left is the positioning.

## How the member reads the result

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| A stranger repeats it back | It is clear. Move on. |
| It fits a competitor's site too | It is generic. Cut more. |
| They cannot say it out loud without it sounding odd | It is written, not spoken. Say it in their words. |

## What to write

Save to `ACX/outputs/positioning.md`: the five blanks, the category chosen and why, the positioning statement, the three one-liners with their test results, and the words cut. Put their one-liner at the top.

Then stop. The next step is testing that message on real buyers' terms.

## Things you must never do

- Write a statement that would fit any audience. If it fits everyone it guides nothing.
- Claim uniqueness they cannot support.
- Use the words every competitor uses.
- Name the market leader and attack them. That only reminds buyers who the leader is.
- Invent a belief for them. If they cannot say it, the positioning is not ready.
